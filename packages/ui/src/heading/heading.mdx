# Heading

A title whose `level` sets its tag, `<h1>` to `<h6>`. Base UI has no heading:
the component builds on the native elements, so the page outline that screen
readers navigate comes from a required prop, never from a tag picked for its
look.

<Canvas of={HeadingStories.Default} />

## Usage

<Guidelines>
  <Guideline when="To title a page, a region or a group of content.">
    One `level={1}` per page, then one level deeper per nested section.
  </Guideline>
</Guidelines>

Not every bold text is a heading:

- To emphasize a label or a value: use the app's text component, which does
  not enter the outline.
- To make a title look bigger or smaller: change its look in the app, never
  its `level`.

## Examples

### Levels

`level` goes from `1` to `6` and renders the matching tag, announced as a
heading of that level.

<Canvas of={HeadingStories.Levels} />

## Keyboard

None: a heading is never focusable. Screen readers jump between headings with
their own keys.

## Accessibility

- `level` is required, so every heading takes its place in the outline on
  purpose.
- Levels are not skipped: an `h2` section holds `h3` titles, not `h4`.
- The title comes from the app's catalog, as `children`.

## API

### Props

The props of a native `<h1>`, plus `level`:

<Controls of={HeadingStories.Default} />

### Attributes

None: `level` changes the tag, `<h1>` to `<h6>`, and no attribute. An app
styles the heading from its own props, never from the tag.

### Styling

The component has no class. Each app, in its own `components/ui/heading.tsx`,
gives it its typography roles, picked by a prop of its own and never derived
from `level`: two headings of the same level can look different, and two of
different levels alike.
